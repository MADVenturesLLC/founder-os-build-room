WHAT I RELY ON
The production write path is the control plane's JournalStore, reached
by POST /journal/commands behind the shared control-plane token. Before
any database contact it normalizes and redacts the envelope
(normalizeForJournal) and refuses the whole row when
containsCredentialMaterial finds a named credential shape in any
caller-supplied field; the route answers 400 credential_material and
echoes nothing. Its identity latch refuses every append unless the
session is br_app_runtime, so today, with the runtime connected as
neondb_owner, no production append is possible. The Tranche D cutover
is the moment that changes. The builder read these from the bound
files on 2026-10-03; the production boot log of Railway deployment
86f8a579 (commit c292c805) reports, at 2026-10-03T11:41:47Z, role
neondb_owner and privilegeAudit pending_cutover.

On 2026-10-03 at 21:41Z the builder ran both shape lists against
synthetic vectors. Of the six shapes in the registry, four are detected
and redacted by the guard; two, aws-access-key-id and slack-token, pass
it neither detected nor redacted. The guard carries shapes the registry
lacks: labelled secrets, Bearer tokens, URL userinfo credentials,
fine-grained GitHub tokens, xAI keys. Neither list contains the other.
That is the drift C3 named.

C3 — RULING
The two lists are reconciled by designation, not by stacking. The
pre-write guard in packages/journal/src/redact.ts, applied in
JournalStore before any database contact, is the redaction boundary of
the production journal write path. JournalAppendSink is not wired into
the journal store and shall not be stacked on the guard. A keyed
boundary may replace the guard only under a later act that names it.

One condition binds this designation. The guard's detection list shall
contain every shape in packages/redaction's registry. The builder adds
aws-access-key-id and slack-token as detection shapes, which only
tightens the guard, and lands a parity test holding the guard to the
registry: for every registry shape, a vector matching it is detected
by containsCredentialMaterial and redacted by normalization. A registry
shape added later that the guard does not detect fails that test.

The header of packages/journal/src/redact.ts and the "Still not wired"
paragraph of packages/redaction/README.md are to cite this ruling as
the reconciliation they say is a Founder act. That edit changes no
behaviour.

FD-3 — RULING
No HMAC key is provisioned for the journal write path, on Railway or
anywhere else. Addendum 02 section 5 option (a), REDACTION_HMAC_KEY as
a sealed Railway variable with an EnvHmacKeyCustody class and the B-N3
boundary, is not commissioned; my 2026-09-27 ruling already withdrew
B-N3 for this write path. Option (c), refusing on Railway, is rejected
because the journal must write in production. The posture is option
(b) as the landed code now makes it: the pre-write guard, not a keyed
boundary, stands at the writer. The Keychain item mad.redaction.hmac /
hmac-v1 for the run-harness sinks is untouched and stays governed by
the Lane B wiring act. FD-3 reopens only with an act that wires a
keyed boundary at the journal writer.

EFFECT ON TRANCHE D
The condition in my 2026-09-27 ruling, "No Tranche D cutover (moving
DATABASE_URL to br_app_runtime) occurs until the JournalAppendSink
reconciliation named in the correction note's C3 has been ruled and
FD-3 key custody has been resolved," is discharged when both hold:
this ruling is landed in custody per CUSTODY below, and the shapes and
parity test of C3's condition are on main. Until both hold, the
condition stands. The 2026-09-27 ruling is otherwise unaltered and its
file is not edited. Discharging the condition authorizes nothing: the
grants migration before the cutover remains subject to Gate IV under
its own act, and the cutover to Gate V under PR 2b implementation plan
r1 section 9 and its own SHA-named act.

I accept, with this ruling, that once the cutover occurs any holder of
the control-plane token can append to the production journal with this

permanent.
